# Syahar — UK Market Intelligence Report #1
**Cross-border caregiver platform · UK corridor deep-dive**
**Prepared:** 2026-06-16 · **Analyst:** CEO office · **Cadence:** refreshed every few hours
**FX used:** £1 ≈ NPR 175 · NPR 1 cr = 10,000,000 · (USD 1 ≈ NPR 133)

> **Methodology note:** This is built on **verified secondary data** (UK Census/ONS, World Bank Remittance Prices, Nepal home-care market pricing) plus a transparent analyst model. It is **not** primary polling — no live survey of UK Nepalis was fielded. Every modelled number states its assumption so you can pressure-test it. A survey instrument to validate the soft assumptions is proposed in §8.

## 1. Executive summary (the shrewd take)

The UK is the right **beachhead corridor — but not for the reason the raw "90,000 Nepalis" headline suggests, and not centred where you'd first guess (Aldershot).**

1. **The headline market is real but smaller than it looks for *our* product.** ~81k Nepal-born / ~107k ethnic Nepali in the UK — but a large, Gurkha-linked share **brought their elderly parents *to* the UK** under post-2009 settlement rights. Those families don't need a caregiver *in Nepal*. **Our true addressable UK segment is the work-/study-migrant cohort (NHS, hospitality, tech, students) whose parents remain in Nepal** — concentrated in **London (Hounslow, Hillingdon, Ealing, Woolwich) and Reading/Basingstoke/Swindon**, not the Gurkha heartland of Rushmoor.
2. **Willingness-to-pay headroom is enormous.** Managed care in Nepal costs roughly **NPR ~9,000–72,000/mo** (basic daily visit → 24/7). Our planning ACV of NPR 32,000/mo ≈ **~£183/mo** — trivial against UK incomes and ~10–30× cheaper than equivalent UK domiciliary care (£20–30/hr). The price objection is essentially zero; the **trust** objection is everything.
3. **The UK Nepali community is unusually primed.** A meaningful share work *in UK social care / NHS* — they already understand and value professional caregiving. That lowers the education burden and makes testimonials credible.
4. **Implication:** keep the UK-first bet, but **re-aim it inside the UK** — lead with London migrant networks + student/NHS-worker channels, treat Rushmoor as a *brand/credibility* play (Gurkha trust halo) rather than the primary demand pool.

## 4. Unit economics (validated against real Nepal pricing)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Avg gross care spend / placement / mo | NPR 32,000 (~**£183**) — sits between basic and 24/7 Nepal pricing ✔ realistic |
| Syahar take (Phase 1, 22%) | NPR 7,040/mo (~£40) |
| Avg placement duration | 2.5 months |
| **Net revenue / placement** | **~NPR 17,600 (~£100)** |
| Caregiver pass-through wage | flows family→carer (not Syahar cost); NPR ~41k/mo wage supports the NPR 32k ACV at part-time/daily intensity |

**Price-resistance read:** at ~£183/mo gross to fully manage a parent's in-hospital/home care — vs UK domiciliary care at £20–30/**hour** — the UK buyer faces a ~10–30× cost advantage. **WTP is not the constraint; trust and proof are.**

## 6. Marketing recommendations (UK)

1. **Re-aim within the UK:** prioritise **London migrant networks (Hounslow/Hillingdon/Ealing/Woolwich) + Reading/Basingstoke** and **student / NHS-worker cohorts** — the families whose parents are *in Nepal*. Use **Rushmoor/Gurkha** networks for **credibility and testimonials** (trust halo), not as the primary demand pool.
2. **Lead with trust, not price.** The £183 figure barely needs defending; spend the message budget on vetting, daily reports, insurance, and the money-back-first-placement guarantee.
3. **Channel priority:** NRNA UK + Gurkha associations (warm, high-trust) → Nepali NHS/care-worker WhatsApp & Facebook groups → student societies (NRN student forums) → small paid-social test. Co-opt the social-care-worker identity ("you care for Britain's elderly; let us care for your own").
4. **Re-balance the Meta test (ties to the open question):** at a £37/mo test budget, spread evenly to *learn*, then concentrate — don't pre-bet 30% on Rushmoor-style targeting.
5. **Proof flywheel:** convert the first London/Reading placements into consented testimonials fast; they're the cheapest acquisition asset.

## 7. Key risks / watch-items
- **Gurkha-elders-in-UK erosion** of the Rushmoor segment (modelled, not yet measured) — could shrink TAM if our 45% "parent in Nepal" rate is optimistic.
- **Trust ceiling:** sending a stranger into a parent's hospital room from 4,500 miles away is a high-trust ask; adoption pace, not WTP, is the risk.
- **FX/remittance reframing risk:** must stay a *caregiver* brand (Golden Rule), not drift into the crowded, low-margin remittance space.

## 8. What primary research would resolve (survey design, ready to field)
If you can field a panel (NRNA lists, Gurkha associations, Nepali UK FB groups), this 6-question instrument resolves the model's biggest unknowns:
1. Do you have a parent/close elder currently living in Nepal? (validates the 45%)
2. Their age band? Any current health/mobility needs? (validates near-term TAM + acute rate)
3. Today, who looks after them when they're hospitalised? (sizes the "informal attendant" incumbent)
4. Monthly £ you'd pay for a vetted, insured caregiver with daily reports? (price ladder £50/100/150/200+)
5. Biggest worry about arranging care from the UK? (trust vs cost vs logistics)
6. Would a money-back-first-placement guarantee change your willingness to try? (conversion lever)
