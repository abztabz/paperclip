02 The problem

The money gets home. Care — and proof of it — does not.

  • 3.5M+ Nepalis work abroad; their parents age at home, and Nepali hospitals expect a family attendant at every in-patient bed.
  • The diaspora child sends remittance hoping it buys care — with no contract, no backup, and no way to know their parent is actually safe.
  • Distance becomes guilt and dread: "Is Aama okay today — and am I failing her by not being there?"
  • Today's "solution" is a relative phoning around for an informal attendant.

04 The solution

An asset-light layer of verified care

  • Caregivers, vetted & insured. A recruited, trained, background-checked pool — with an on-call backup bench so no patient is ever unattended.
  • Every visit is verified. Daily care reports, vitals, and consented photo/video — turned into clear "your mother ate, walked, was well" updates.
  • Pay & manage from abroad. In-app cross-border payment in home currency, caregiver profiles, messaging, scheduled calls.
  • The family, together. Siblings share one feed; one transparent NPR bill; money-back on the first placement.
How it works

1. Child abroad picks a care plan & pays in-app.

2. A vetted caregiver attends the parent — hospital or home.

3. Verified daily report + health notes after each visit.

4. Family sees the feed, messages, video-calls.

⭐ Golden rule Syahar is a caregiver platform, never a remittance / money-transfer service. Payment partners (IME, eSewa, Khalti) are rails, never our product.

05 Beachhead · why the UK first

Win the highest-trust, highest-paying corridor first

Our research re-aimed the entry point. The UK isn't the biggest diaspora — it's the best beachhead: settled, high-income, smartphone-native families whose parents are still in Nepal, many already working in UK health & social care.

  • WTP headroom is huge. ~£183/mo to fully manage a parent vs £20–30/hr UK care — price is not the objection; trust is.
  • Care-fluent audience. Many UK Nepalis work in the NHS/social care — they already value professional caregiving.
  • Dense trust networks. NRNA UK + Gurkha associations give warm, high-credibility reach.
  • Then expand down every remittance corridor — Gulf, Australia, USA — where the diaspora is far larger.

Sources: ONS 2021 Census; Rushmoor BC; World Bank UK→Nepal corridor (~3.5% cost). Within-UK targeting = London (Hounslow/Hillingdon/Ealing/Woolwich) + Reading/Basingstoke.

07 Competitive landscape · the honest read

Carers exist. A cross-border trust layer does not.

We are not claiming an empty market — that would be naïve. Nepal home-care providers exist. None is a UK-diaspora-first, app-mediated platform with vetting, daily proof, FX billing and hospital MoUs. That gap is the wedge — and the window is open, not infinite.

The field today

  • MedEx, Nursing Care Nepal, Doctors House Call — Nepal home-nursing; one already courts NRN families.
  • RNest — an app entrant in the Nepal care space.
  • Informal attendants — the disorganised status quo.

Why Syahar wins

  • The cross-border experience: UK-side onboarding, daily reports, home-currency billing, concierge.
  • A switching-cost moat: the family health record + daily-report habit + caregiver loyalty.
  • Build-or-partner on supply: existing agencies can be suppliers, not rivals — we own the diaspora relationship.

16 Risks we design around

What could kill this — and our answer

Risk

  • Serious care incident — one case is brand-radioactive.
  • Trust ceiling — a stranger in a parent's room from 4,500 miles.
  • Caregiver supply can't meet demand.
  • Disintermediation — family pays the carer directly.
  • Competitor / app entrant moves on the corridor.

Our answer

  • Vetting, insurance, medical-scope limits, fast escalation SOPs.
  • Manual concierge, real daily proof, money-back first placement.
  • Training pipeline, referral incentives, dignity-led retention.
  • Own the trust layer + recurring proof + family health record.
  • Speed on the diaspora moat; partner agencies for supply.
