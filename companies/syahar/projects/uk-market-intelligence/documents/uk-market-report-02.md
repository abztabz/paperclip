## 1. Competitive landscape (NEW — the important section)

The Nepal eldercare *delivery* market is **more populated than the original plan assumed:**

| Player | What they do | Diaspora-facing? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nursing Care Nepal** | Matches certified nurses to elders; trial week, no lock-in; **explicitly references "NRN families"** | **Yes — direct** | [nursingcarenepal.com](https://nursingcarenepal.com/blogs/complete-guide-elderly-home-care-kathmandu) |
| **MedEx (Nurse-at-Home)** | Registered nurses / aides for bed-bound & **hospitalised** patients | Partly | [medex.com.np](https://medex.com.np/service/medex-nurse-at-home/) |
| **Doctors House Call** | Home nursing, ~NPR 100/hr | Domestic | [doctorshousecall.com.np](https://doctorshousecall.com.np/blogs/home-nursing-care-service-in-nepal/) |
| **Naman Care Home / Heal Home Care** | Residential assisted living (est. 2018) | Domestic | search |
| **Hope Hermitage** | Elderly care services | Domestic | [hopehermitage.org.np](https://hopehermitage.org.np/services/) |
| **RNest (iOS app)** | App in the Nepal care space | Digital | [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/rnest/id6755589286) |

**Analyst read:**
- The **supply/delivery layer is contested** (nurses, home-care, even hospital attendants exist). Syahar does *not* have a clear field there.
- The **cross-border demand layer is still relatively open** — none of the above is a UK-diaspora-first, trust-engineered, app-mediated platform with daily reporting + home-currency billing + UK-side concierge. **That is the wedge to defend.**
- But **Nursing Care Nepal explicitly markets to NRN families**, and **RNest signals an app entrant** — so the window is open, not infinite. **Speed on the diaspora-trust moat matters.**

**Strategic implications:**
1. **Drop "no competitors" from all messaging** (it's false and brittle). Compete on *trust + cross-border experience*, not novelty.
2. **Partner-or-compete option:** existing home-care providers (MedEx, Nursing Care Nepal) could be **supply partners** rather than rivals — Syahar owns the diaspora relationship + coordination; they supply vetted carers. Worth a build-vs-partner decision for the supply side.
3. **Defensive moat = the family health record + daily-report habit + caregiver loyalty** (already in the 5-yr plan) — accelerate these; they're the switching costs competitors can't easily copy.

## 2. Income / WTP refinement
- **Gurkha Welfare pensions:** 3,812 veterans/widows, **51% aged 80+**, receiving ~NPR 13,100/mo — but these are recipients **in Nepal**, not UK-settled earners ([Gurkha Welfare Trust](https://www.gwt.org.uk/news/annual-review-21-22/)). Reinforces that the *low-income elderly* are a different group from the *UK-earning adult children* who are our buyers.
- **Two-speed community confirmed:** higher-earning work/study migrants (our buyers) vs lower-income/elderly Gurkha-linked households. **The buyer is the working-age migrant child, not the pensioner** — consistent with #1's London-over-Rushmoor re-aim.
- Nepal avg income ~NPR 28,000/mo (~USD 240) — context for caregiver wages and the affordability gap that makes UK-funded care transformational for the carer side.
